## The experience to pursue

**Take turns being your partner's way through.** Isp and Pac escape an unruly living
machine by making a route for each other, swapping responsibility inside a puzzle,
and turning the villains' tricks into useful opportunities. The memorable moment is
"I've got this; now you go", followed by a victory neither character could earn alone.
Simple movement, readable consequences, cute molecular heroes, and mischievous body
horror give that cooperation personality. Biology provides the setting and shapes.

Use **Fix the design, not the symptom**: deepen causal cooperation rather than add
walking, faster hazards, or more switches with familiar answers. A supported bridge
is interesting when choosing who supports it changes what the partner can do. A
villain attack is interesting when inviting, avoiding, or interrupting it changes a
route. Each chapter should introduce, vary, then combine an idea, with breathing
rooms between demanding relays. **Use the scientific method**: playable changes and
observed attempts decide whether this direction succeeds.

## Reassess after each change

For each integrated batch, first prove legal ordinary-input completion and a
plausible missed-jump/early-release recovery on the actual build. Review the changed
rooms beside adjacent campaign rooms; confirm that another repeated relay has not
replaced the proposed novelty. Recheck progression, both roles, and the ending after
shared mechanism changes; rerun the full campaign when shared behavior changes.

Then observe two people discovering the room without route coaching. Record each
player's decisions, explanations of the partner's effect, idle periods, deaths,
restarts, help, and solved setup replayed. Distinguish coordination from confusion
and purposeful suspense from carrier waiting. Ask what they expected to happen and
when they felt useful. Also keep single-operator evidence separate from two-player
play. Human enjoyment and novice difficulty remain open until that observation.

Promote a candidate when it creates a recognizable cooperative moment with clear
consequences; revise or remove it when it merely adds time, memorized instructions,
or one player's busywork. Reassess these priorities after the finale and added-room
results instead of treating this document as a fixed master plan.
