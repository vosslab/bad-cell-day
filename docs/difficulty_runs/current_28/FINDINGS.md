## Challenge, clarity and recovery

Ten rooms carry clarity3, while the remaining rooms carry clarity2. This is an evidence-backed
question about testing causal links and reading overlapping cues, not proof of ten unusable rooms.
Cargo Exchange has a visible rider/load panel, but CARGO PARKED needs cargo plus a hero and the
return depends on unloading one side before reweighting the other. That is worthwhile reasoning
if feedback distinguishes missing rider, equal load and unopened return access. A larger score
alone does not tell us which part a first pair will misread.

The liver rooms expose a more specific cue problem: START/BUBBLES/LIFT can start attacks
persistently, while associated HOLD/CROSS gates still require occupancy until their appropriate
release. FLOAT is useful, Pac side contact consumes a shot, and top contact preserves it. The
safe floors at positions 15, 16, 18 and 19 let players test those distinctions without death.
Retain that protection and the alternate solutions; improve the legibility of relevant changes
if unassisted attempts show players cannot connect them. More explanatory text is not yet the
evidenced solution.

Common mistakes separate into two costs. Releasing a holder, pausing an unpowered solid carrier,
missing a safe-floor FLOAT or arriving at a still-closed gate often permits reoccupation, another
shot, or reboarding with latches retained. Falling into slime, the bomb, or a lethal gap creates a
fresh room and loses local cargo/relay setup. RESCUE is durable within an attempt, not a death
checkpoint. Counting every temporary effect as a hazard would erase this useful distinction.
Finale charge breaks reset only charge; a late lethal fall loses the preceding relay and earns
recovery3. That makes late recovery comprehension a stronger question than merely adding speed.

Completed current-source recoveries in A Meeting of Minds, Return to Sender and Attention,
Please! are useful evidence of room resilience. They do not prove every arbitrary cargo/fall
state recoverable. The known Pac919 exit endpoint in A Meeting of Minds is a disclosed scripted
controller adaptation: an ordinary left step completes. It is not evidence that humans find
the room difficult or that the product requires a geometry patch.

## Both heroes and villain play

The strongest co-op structures change who enables whom. Joint RESTORE requires both bodies;
heavy cargo needs connected pushers; Cargo Exchange needs riders and opposite ballast; Crossed
Paths repeatedly returns access; I've Got You gives Pac an upper route from Isp's FLOAT and Isp
a lower route released by Pac. A Meeting of Minds makes Pac choose carrier position remotely,
then makes Isp release Pac's exit route. These are meaningful contributions beyond two exits.
Preserve their distinct paths and reciprocal work when expanding the campaign.

Not every good tactic has to be mandatory. Bubble Bouncer's interception, Two for One's shared
flight, Attention, Please!'s deliberate decoy, and the finale's optional FLOAT/RESCUE choices
offer room for partners to find different methods. Blocking these freedoms to enforce an authored
recipe would discard useful agency without evidence of a gameplay problem. A future room can
add a new cooperative use rather than replacing a successful room or requiring every room to use
the same partner pattern.

Gem and Cana already recur through projectiles, native bridge/power sabotage, dialogue and
temporary reactions. Safe-floor FLOAT/STICKY/DIZZY/SHOVE can interrupt or become routing tools;
near lethal ground displacement has a different consequence. Rescue interruptions are brief and
bound to particular controls, not universal permanent villain shutdown. Keep their recurrence and
make new interactions readable enough for intentional use, escape or playful risk. Unpredictable
play can come from changing partner choices and committed attack windows; random disruption is
not necessary to claim it.

Gem's Liver Lair is a concrete contrast. Actual assembled `level_16` has `interference: []` and
no initial interference state; bridge collapse and ferry power cycling remain real sabotage.
Its target pads overlap the exits and cause immediate defeat without a charge timer. Consequently
the boss adds arrival feedback to an already familiar relay rather than a separate playable
countermove. The viewed PNG shows a purple robot-like central boss labeled GEM and the distinct
molecular Gem at upper left, plus crowded far TARGET/RESCUE/EXIT labels. This is an identity and
interaction opportunity, not grounds to inflate novelty or invent absent attacks. See
[LEVEL_16.md](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_16.md) for source/evidence detail.
