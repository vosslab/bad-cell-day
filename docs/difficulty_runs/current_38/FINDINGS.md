# Current38 findings

This assessment summarizes source-aware estimates for the frozen 38-room campaign. Puzzle and platforming axes are ordinal judgments, not measures of player success, discovery, enjoyment, time, or frustration. See the [campaign report](report/LEVEL_DIFFICULTY_REPORT.md) and [campaign map](campaign_map.json).

## Campaign shape

The opening teaches hold, steady, stop, lift, and rescue roles. Later rooms reuse them with new pad effects or release roles, supporting transfer alongside repeated hold-and-latch relays. Positions 9-13 (The Great Cell Shuffle through Elevator Etiquette) mostly sit around a 68 puzzle axis, with Split Decision at 50; this is a run of related ferry, bridge, crate, and lift work rather than a steady ramp. Position 9, `level_05`, uses STOP ferry -> Pac STEADY -> Isp RESCUE; it has no cargo. The 57 platforming profile there reflects active ferry and bridge crossing, not a cargo-delivery sequence. See the [level 5 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_05.md).

The clearest puzzle-profile peak is position 14, **Cargo Exchange** (`level_23`): puzzle 100, platforming 32. Both riders control counterweight motion while the pair exchanges cargo to send the decks out and back; the visible WAIT FOR BOTH cue explains the rider condition, but not how relative load selects direction or the full return sequence. Safe docks and repositioning reduce routine recovery cost, so the high puzzle estimate comes from its coupled model and role exchange rather than punishing movement. Position 26, **Gem's Liver Lair** (`level_16`), is another spike at 82 puzzle and 54 platforming: each hero's different pad and release order controls the shared lift/route, with a simultaneous target condition. These are distinct challenges worth preserving. Neither has a retained human route observation, so the profiles identify useful observation targets, not proof that players struggle. See the [level 23 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_23.md) and [level 16 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_16.md).

There are also clear breathers. **Narrow Escape** at position 6 is 43 puzzle / 18 platforming after the recoil lesson at position 5; **Split Decision** at position 11 is 50 / 29; **Gem's Bubble Break** at position 18 is 50 / 29. In these rooms, familiar role cues, forgiving geometry, or a short handoff keep the mandatory route modest. These breathers can help the campaign breathe; they need no added hazards or jumps. Later rooms vary more: position 24, **Two for One** (`level_28`), uses a partner-stacked jump so Pac bounces the still-live bubble and Isp can FLOAT on that same bubble; it scores 75 / 54. Position 29, **Return to Sender** (`level_25`), reuses parcel and return ideas at 68 / 39. Position 35, **Changing Places** (`level_29`), and position 36, **No Takebacks** (`level_33`), also reuse delivery and reciprocal gate roles, with lower confidence in the latter source-aware estimates. Recombination can still be worthwhile, but repeated parcel/latch loops are a less promising expansion direction than a new causal model.

Meaningful two-hero work appears at several scales. Position 3, **Bridge Over Troubled Slime** (`level_04`), requires Pac to hold the bridge steady while Isp reaches RESCUE, then releases Pac to cross; breaking the order risks a lethal fall and a full restart. Position 14 requires simultaneous rider occupancy and a two-way role exchange. Position 37, **Cana's Control Freak-Out** (`level_19`), teaches the STOP/STEADY/RESCUE sequence and paired targets. Position 38, **A Very Bad Day for the Tumor** (`level_20`), extends that finale: Isp's high blue TARGET takes over bridge support and opens Pac's route before both heroes hold separate targets for the final charge. The finale has a high puzzle profile (86), high platforming profile (68), and recovery burden (3). Preserve this handoff and payoff; the assessment gives no basis to flatten it. See the [level 19 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_19.md) and [level 20 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_20.md).

## Clarity and recovery

The campaign map keeps clarity and recovery separate from its two axes. That distinction matters: position 1, **Hold That Door!** (`level_02`), has low recovery cost (1) but clarity burden 3; a safe retry does not mean the route is immediately clear. Positions 3 and 4 (`level_04` and `level_03`) each have recovery burden 3 because a plausible mistake can cause a lethal reset. Cargo Exchange has clarity burden 3 but recovery 2 because most wrong turns stop progress or permit repositioning. The finale also has recovery 3 because a lethal mistake repeats a long sequence. Treat these as different design questions: improve a cue when the causal relation is hard to infer, and improve recovery only where a plausible error repeats substantial work.

The first pass used idle-only captures. Proposed actions and source-aware routes are not observed player actions, and a later score change is a comparison of evidence and hindsight, not a causal measure of expert bias or human learning. Three passive animation changes in positions 14, 15, and 17 (`level_23`, `level_12`, `level_36`) have unknown causes. First-pass blindness remained intact; however, the source-aware author for position 4 (`level_03`) had seen its historical score before writing that record, limiting independence for that room. The map retains low confidence at positions 33, 35, and 36. Only five rooms have current native-input traces, at positions 3, 5, 9, 15, and 34; they establish scripted feasibility for those inputs only. All 38 control-friction values remain unknown. None of this is human play evidence. See the [run ledger](README.md) and the [rubric](../../DIFFICULTY_RUBRIC.md).

## Ranked next work

1. **Clarify role-card scope and position 10's release order.** The team cards describe Isp as stopping machinery on blue pads and Pac as steadying orange platforms, though pads can also open doors, latch routes, or power lifts. In **Double Trouble** (`level_07`, position 10), Pac's STEADY pad also brakes the far ferry. Pac must hold until Isp boards, then release it toward RESCUE. The title and subtitle signal patience, but not this causal order; the optional hint states it. Tighten the general role descriptions and add a visible hold-until-boarded cue. This is a source-backed clarity opportunity, not evidence that a human pair has already been confused. See the [team cards](../../../src/app.tsx#L400) and [level 7 source](../../../src/levels_middle.ts#L144).
2. **Resolve the reported side-to-side motion and easy falls at position 3.** The authored bridge has no horizontal motion; accepted acid-push work preserved its geometry and timing. That does not disprove what the user reported seeing. The existing ordinary route and optional villain-dunk traces do not isolate the report. Reproduce it against the current consumer and record bridge position and hero contacts before choosing a change. Keep the optional dunk separate from the mandatory route estimate. See the [level 4 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_04.md) and [level 4 source](../../../src/levels_tutorial.ts#L229).
3. **Observe a first-time pair in Cargo Exchange, then use the result to choose an expansion direction.** Its two-deck load model is the strongest puzzle spike, and the written source route establishes feasibility but not discoverability. A short observation can show which load-direction and return cues need help. For expansion, explore a distinct paired causal model, building on the campaign's recoil and shove ideas, instead of adding another hold-relay or parcel-transfer loop by default. Current estimates identify room for a different mechanic; they do not establish a specific new room or mechanic as necessary. See the [level 23 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_23.md), [level 37 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_37.md), and [level 36 dossier](report/difficulty_levels/LEVEL_36.md).

These priorities follow likely player impact and evidence strength. Score spikes identify good places to inspect; the low-profile rooms and accepted finale show why difficulty should be judged by the kind of work and its recovery, not by making every room harder.
